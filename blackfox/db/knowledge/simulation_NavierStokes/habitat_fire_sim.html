Habitat Fire Spread

A 3D fire model of a 6 m × 3 m × 2 m two-deck habitat with six rooms. The air is solved with variable-density Navier–Stokes equations, using large-eddy turbulence, buoyancy and thermal expansion. Fuel vapour burns where it mixes with oxygen. Flames on the furnishings follow a flame-temperature model fitted to NASA BASS-II, Saffire and ISS PMMA data, then checked against LUCI Lunar-gravity results. Everything runs in your browser.

    The model

    Each step (Δt up to 0.25 s, about two cell crossings) solves these equations on the 3D grid. They follow the structure of NIST's Fire Dynamics Simulator, simplified where the browser needs it.

    1. Momentum, with variable-density buoyancy and SGS turbulence. ∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p̃ + g (T/T₀ − 1) ĵ + ∇·[(ν + νt)∇u], νt = (0.18 Δ)² |S|

      Full density-ratio buoyancy (ρ₀ − ρ)/ρ, not Boussinesq. Smagorinsky large-eddy closure. Semi-Lagrangian advection.

    2. Pressure projection with thermal expansion (low-Mach constraint). ∇·u = q̇‴net / (ρ cp T), ∇²p̃ = (∇·u* − ∇·u) / Δt

      Red-black SOR solve. The supply is a velocity inlet and the return a pressure outlet. Sealed rooms keep the mean expansion as a background pressure rise, dP/dt = (γ − 1) Q̇/V. This is how a closed hatch traps pressure and smoke.

    3. Species and energy. YO₂, fuel vapour YF (in O₂-equivalent mass), soot Ys and T, each advected and diffused with Prt = Sct = 0.5. q̇‴ = ρ ΔHO₂ min(YF, YO₂)/τmix, ΔHO₂ = 13.1 MJ/kg, χr = 0.30, loss 4κσ(T⁴ − T₀⁴) + hw(T − T₀)

      Mixing-controlled combustion with Huggett's constant. Bulk gas burns only above 700 K, and only if a stoichiometric mixture can reach the 1600 K critical flame temperature (FDS-style extinction). Soot extinction coefficient 8700 m²/kg, visibility = 3/K.

    4. Flame temperature at a burning surface (radiative quench / kinetic blowoff). Tf = Tg + κ XO₂ [1 − R (101.3/P)/(U + Ud)], flame lives if Tf ≥ Tc and U ≤ Ubo = Ubo,ref·√(P X/(101.3·21))·e−(Ta/2)(1/Tf − 1/Tref)

      U = √(Uair² + Ub²) with Ub = √(g L (1 − Tg/Tf)) (Froude scaling, L = 1.2 cm at the flame base). R and Tc are fitted to the BASS-II quench speeds, and Ubo,ref to the 1 g downward limits. This reproduces the U-shaped maps of Olson & Ferkul and T'ien-type theory.

    5. Flame spread over thin solids. Vopp = (π/4) λg(Tf − Tv) / [m″ cs (Tv − Tg)] · √(1 − U/Ubo), Vcon = kc Us Φ(Tf) √(1 − U/Ubo,c)

      Opposed spread is de Ris thin-fuel theory with Delichatsios' exact π/4 factor. Concurrent spread is linear in flow (BASS), with kc fitted to Saffire-1 and scaled to other materials by m″cs(Tv − T₀). Us uses the burning length above the flame, so upward fires speed up in gravity.

    6. Solid heating, piloted ignition and burning. m″cs dTs/dt = h(Tg − Ts) + εgσ(Tg⁴ − Ts⁴) + q″nbr flame − εσ(Ts⁴ − T₀⁴), ṁ″ = (q″fl + q″layer)/Lg

      A hot smoke layer heats furnishings in the next room until they pyrolyse at Tv. They ignite once the local flame-temperature test passes. Fuel vapour enters the gas cell and burns there, releasing the heat.

    Methods and data sources

    • Results of Large-Scale Spacecraft Flammability Tests (Saffire-1: 1.8 mm/s at 20 cm/s; 2.1 mm/s narrow strip)
    • BASS/BASS-II concurrent flame growth and quenching over SIBAL fabric
    • LUCI: Lunar Combustion Investigation flammability results (2025)
    • Ferkul et al., LPSC 2026: Lunar gravity near the most flammable condition
    • NASA FM2: Flammability of Materials on the Moon
    • The Effect of Gravity on Flame Spread over PMMA Cylinders (ISS, 17% vs 18% O₂)
    • Marcum 2016: 1 g and 0 g blowoff limits correlated by buoyant stretch
    • Saffire V opposed spread along structured PMMA (0.88 mm/s)
    • Olson & Ferkul: Martian-gravity drop-tower flammability
    • Microgravity flame spread in exploration atmospheres (0 g limits up to 4% below 1 g)
    • Theoretical flammability bounds for thin fuels: radiative extinction and blowoff
    • Bhattacharjee et al.: opposed-flow flame spread theory and extinction criteria
    • Exact solution for creeping flame spread over thin materials (π/4 factor)
    • McGrattan et al.: FDS combustion and extinction modelling
    • LES flame extinction with fast chemistry and a critical flame temperature

    What this model still cannot do

    • It is not FDS. Cells of 8–12.5 cm cannot resolve a millimetre-thick flame, so surface flames are subgrid models fitted to the data in the validation table.
    • Moon and Mars results remain predictions. Only LUCI (about 2.5 minutes) and short drop-tower and aircraft tests exist at partial gravity, until FM2 flies.
    • Two independent checks disagree, and the table shows both. Edge spread on PMMA ribs is about 6× faster than flat-sheet theory predicts. The model puts the gravity of easiest burning at 0.06 g, against roughly 0.16 g measured.
    • Nomex limits are assumed, not fitted. There is no CO or HCN chemistry and no suppression agent.
