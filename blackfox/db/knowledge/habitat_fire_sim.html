        The mathematical model
        
    
        Each time step of 0.1 s solves these equations on a 120 × 60 grid of 5 cm cells. Equations 1–3 describe the cabin air. Equations 4–7 describe the burning solids.
        
    
          
      
        1. Ventilation flow (potential flow).
        ∇²φ = 0 in cabin air;  φ = 1 at supply, φ = 0 at return, ∂φ/∂n = 0 on walls and closed hatches;  u = −c ∇φ
        
          c is set so the air leaves the supply diffuser at the chosen speed. Solved by SOR (ω = 1.9). It is re-solved whenever a door or hatch changes.
          2. 
      
        2. Buoyant rise of hot gas.
        wb = Ub · min(1, θ / 900 K),   Ub = 30 cm/s · √(g / gE) · (P / 101.3 kPa)
        
          The 30 cm/s value is the order-of-magnitude buoyant flow near a flame on Earth (curated fact). The pressure term follows Gr ∝ g·P² (low pressure as a proxy for low g, pmc-6907022). At 0 g it vanishes, so smoke and heat move only with the fans.
          3. 
      
        3. Heat, smoke and oxygen transport. θ = gas temperature rise, d = O₂ deficit, s = smoke.
        ∂χ/∂t + ∇·[(u + wb ĵ) χ] = D ∇²χ + Sχ − λχ χ,   χ ∈ {θ, d, s},  D = 15 cm²/s
        
          Each burning cell adds Sθ = 250 K/s × hm × Y/21, Sd = 1.2 %/s × Y/21 and smoke. Heat is lost at λ = 0.01 s⁻¹ (radiation), rising to 0.06 s⁻¹ next to walls. The scheme is explicit finite-volume upwind with CFL ≤ 0.4.
          4. 
      
        4. Flammability limit (fitted to knowledge-base data).
        Ylim(U) = a + b/U + c·U + 4·(1 − P/101.3),   U = √(|u|² + Ub²)
        
          b/U is the quenching side, from BASS-II quench speeds of 1–5 cm/s. c·U is the linear blowoff side (Marcum 2016). There are separate (a, b, c) for concurrent and opposed spread.
          5. 
      
        5. Flame spread over a surface. ψ = angle between the spread direction and the local flow.
        V(ψ) = Vopp + (G·Vcon − Vopp)·cos⁴ψ  (cos ψ ≥ 0),   Vcon = kc·U·Φ·(Y/21)·√(P/P₀),   Vopp = ko·Φ·(Y/21)²·(P/P₀)0.3·h(U)
        
          Φ = 1 − exp(−(Y − Ylim)/3). G = 1 + (Ub/U)·min(L/5 cm, 8) makes buoyant upward fires speed up as they grow. h(U) peaks near 5 cm/s, matching the non-monotonic opposed spread measured on the ISS (pmc-5760683).
          6. 
      
        6. Ignition by hot gas. This is how fire jumps a doorway.
        dξ/dt = max(0, T − 600 K) / (300 K · 8 s),   ignite when ξ ≥ 1
          7. 
      
        7. Extinction and burnout. A burning cell goes out when its local Y < Ylim(Ulocal). This is quenching below the flow optimum √(b/c) and blowoff above it. Otherwise it burns out after tb·21/Y, unless it is still heating the next cell.
          8. 
    
        
  
        

  
        This is a teaching and design-exploration model, not a validated fire code like FDS. The flow is potential flow, not Navier–Stokes. The limit lines are hand-fitted to published trends, and spread rates are order-of-magnitude. Before using a number, open the cited NTRS record and check it against the PSI experiment tables.
