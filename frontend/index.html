01

No buoyancy, no plume.

Without gravity hot gas doesn't rise. The flame turns round, blue and slow, and lives on whatever the ventilation brings it. Stop the fan and it starts to starve — but it may not go out quickly: in Saffire V, at raised oxygen, thin fabrics could not be counted on to go out once the flow stopped.

NTRS 20210017780 · NTRS 20080034883

02

Lower pressure, more oxygen.

Lunar and Martian habitats plan a 56.5 kPa, 34 % oxygen atmosphere. NASA's data show that for most materials the oxygen fraction, not the pressure, decides — and 0.165 g on the Moon is a goldilocks zone: enough buoyancy, little heat loss. In our model polyethylene burns hard here.

NTRS 20160001047 · NTRS 20240015307 · NTRS 20240003211

03

Smoke goes where the air goes.

In orbit smoke does not collect at the ceiling; it follows the ventilation. Detection time is set by the air flow and the distance to the detector; stop the air and the smoke cloud stays where it was born. Nominal ventilation is 5–20 cm/s.

NTRS 20130000422 · NTRS 20080034883

Set up your own fire.

Environment, pressure, oxygen, temperature, material, place in the module, air flow and suppression — the lab recomputes the fire and shows it in 3D: interior, X-ray, thermal and airflow views, flammability map and timeline.

For Businesses

Turn space fire research into actionable engineering decisions.

BLACKFOX helps aerospace engineers, research organizations, and safety teams explore fire risks, compare safety strategies, and make more informed design decisions through research-backed interactive simulation.

Aerospace & Spacecraft Developers

Who it’s for: Spacecraft manufacturers, space habitat developers, and aerospace engineering teams.

  • Evaluate fire safety scenarios during early design.
  • Compare environmental and material configurations.
  • Explore safety interventions before physical testing.
  • Support engineering decisions with simulation results.

Research & Material Engineering

Who it’s for: Research laboratories, universities, and material engineering teams.

  • Explore material flammability under different environmental conditions.
  • Access and interpret relevant NASA fire research.
  • Identify research gaps and promising test scenarios.
  • Support preliminary material screening.

Safety Training & Education

Who it’s for: Astronaut training organizations, educational institutions, and safety teams.

  • Visualize fire behavior in enclosed space environments.
  • Demonstrate the effect of emergency responses.
  • Compare safety strategies interactively.
  • Support scenario-based training and education.
